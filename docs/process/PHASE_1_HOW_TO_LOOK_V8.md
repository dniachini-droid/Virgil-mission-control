## What changed, and why

### The consoles behind Virgil's, symmetrical

Your words: *"each console should be behind the main virgil console, one directly behinmd it and the other to the left and right, so its symmetrical."* Virgil's console stays where it was. The Prover's stands directly behind it, square to you; the Fabricator's to the left and the Keeper's to the right, mirrored exactly and turned a little in. Which role took which slot you did not say; the Fabricator, the Prover and the Keeper are left, centre and right in the order of the three hops, and that is this session's choice. **A consequence you should see rather than be told:** with the camera almost level, whoever stands directly behind Virgil is partly behind him from the opening view — you see the Prover's head and shoulders and his console's screen above Virgil's head, and all of him when you drag round. And a symmetrical set is wide where a phone is tall, so on a phone held upright the cast is a band across the middle of a tall frame; all four are in it.

### The consoles' own screens carry the information; the floating panels are gone

Your words: *"each screen of the actual consoles (created by meshy) should have the information on it"*, and, of the treatment, *"make them compleetyley black, reflective, and text sitting slightly under it."* It is the visor technique on a console. A pipeline script finds the screen on each console's own mesh and records which of its triangles it is; at runtime those triangles are copied out and the live screen is drawn on them through the same face and glass that make the visors — one implementation, two hosts. The glass stands twelve millimetres off the screen so the text sits under it and a highlight travels across it.

**What the consoles turned out to carry, measured:** each has exactly one usable screen — a recessed navy panel in a cream bezel at the top, about 0.9 by 0.55 metres, tilted back thirteen degrees, facing you — with pale writing baked into it. The screens are **navy, not black**, the same navy as the trim, so the visor's paint rule could not find them from the texture; they are selected by geometry (a measured region, the facing, the darkness of most of the triangle). And the baked writing means a per-pixel mask would have punched holes through the content, so the whole selected triangle is the screen, which paints over the writing. Your idea worked; the models just did not honour the "pure black" you asked Meshy for, and it did not matter.

The three floating panels are retired. That, and the agents' screens being dark when idle, is what answers *"too many screens"*.

### No window

Your words, twice: *"remove the circular window. I asked for that."* V7 kept the arch as a skyline element and read your first instruction as the window in the wall; that was wrong, and it is gone. The model stays in `assets/models/candidates/` as evidence, and its payload stays in the file because the retired room's window frame still uses it — dropping it would save about 0.9 MB and is one decision away.

### The camera almost level; the planet and the station moved up

Your words: *"The camera starts too high up so everything looks squished. It should start almost levelm with the characters, maybe a tiny bit higher."* Eleven degrees above chest height, not thirty. The orbit stays on a wide arc, and you can rise to look over the set but not sink under the disc. *"the planet and space station in the background is covered by the floating tabletop, so they need to be moved up"* — both are raised so they clear the disc's far edge from the level camera, and they swapped sides so the planet is not behind the controls.

### The screen animations, rebuilt for craft

Your words, three times over: *"they look a little basic … not something that is basic clip art that is animated"*; *"make the animations more... futeristic. put work into those animations, make it look detailed. And don't make the 'recieving' screen so quick"*; *"the information should come back onto the screen and almost converge, or like millions of pieces of data coming to gether."* No words were added. What changed is the motion: every easing has weight, things arrive in sequence, secondary motion trails primary by a beat, and nothing steps or snaps — V6's rule that motion should be "stepped and snappy" is reversed by your direction. The receiving beat is the centrepiece, six seconds in four stages; the return is its inverse, built as a pair so the two read as a cycle. A verdict is an event now — the ring seals, pulses once, the mark draws itself — not a colour change.

**The seam for your tube idea.** *"Maybe later we can have tubes connecting all of the consoles and … light travels across the tube to the correct console, and back again."* Not built, and worth building: it would make a hand-off one continuous event and give the receiving beat its duration honestly. What is built toward it: every receiving and returning animation starts from a **defined arrival point** — the screen edge nearest Virgil, at a recorded moment — kept in one file (`screens/arrival.ts`), which also gives the point in the room where a tube would terminate on each console. A tube can feed into that without the animations being rebuilt. The symmetrical layout is what makes three tubes from one console natural.

### Counters, real vocabulary, and four verdicts

Your words: *"there should also be a counter for the keeper, for example how many issues it found and blockers it found … same as the proover, like the outcomes of its tests as it works (but it shoudlnt recquire a change in how the actual agent reacts)."* The Keeper's screen counts `FINDINGS` and `BLOCKING`; the Prover's counts `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `RUNNING` and, when one could not run, `SKIPPED` — the check-result words of the constitution; the Fabricator's counts `FILES` and `COMMITS`, the shape of a candidate-artifact record. The faces, the lights and the behaviour are exactly as they were. The numbers are illustrative and every screen says so.

**And a correction of fact.** V7 showed two colours for four verdicts. `constitution/REVIEW_POLICY.md` names four — `PASS`, `PASS_WITH_NON_BLOCKING_FINDINGS`, `BLOCKED`, `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` — and says the last two "are different states with different geometry, never distinguished by colour alone." The two that were collapsed were the ones that matter most: every real review of this repository has returned `PASS_WITH_NON_BLOCKING_FINDINGS`, never a bare pass, and `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` is not a failure. Each now has its own shape as well as its colour, the demonstration's third loop ends in insufficient evidence so all four are seen, and — a consequence for honesty — the Keeper's illustrative review raises three findings, none blocking, so his verdict is `PASS WITH NON-BLOCKING FINDINGS`, not `PASS`.

### The evidence beneath a verdict, and the owner gate

Of the four things offered when you asked what else could show what is actually happening, you chose the first and the third. **The evidence** is on Virgil's centre slab under every verdict: a small number of counts whose kinds are the ones this repository really produces — its tests, its typecheck tasks, the Owner Build's requests and off-document requests, the knowledge graph's tethers — because a verdict alone is a claim and the checks that produced it are the proof. **The owner gate** is a new beat: after the Keeper's verdict the candidate is `SAFE_TO_MERGE` — "every merge gate passes. Eligible. Not merged." — and only you can merge, so the system stops and turns to you. The two you did not choose, the repair budget and the frozen candidate SHA, are kept in the spec as offered and not selected.

### Turning, not walking

Your words: *"have each agent at the console, sightly to the left so it doesnt obstruct the screens, faciung forward. When they get sent work, an animation plays on their face/eyes and they turn aroundfacing the screeen. When its done, they turn aroiund and face the front again."* Built exactly so, and it needs no rig: a whole-body turn on one axis that starts from rest, overshoots the screen by four and a half degrees and settles, the breathing continuing through it; the face flares first, then the body turns. **The three walking rigs you have pending are not needed for this, and nothing waits on them.** The standing point is derived from the console's measured front and the character's widest reach, so no angle of the turn can sweep a shoulder into the console, and a test sweeps the whole turn to prove it.

### The screens dark when idle, on like a television, off like an old one; the consoles spotlit

Your words: *"we should avoid having every screen on if its not in use … when it turns on, make it turn on like a tv, and whene it turns off, an exadurated old tv turning off type animation"*, and *"when they are doing work, they are lit up lighter, almnost as if a spotlight is on them, and when they are done, the spotlitght slowly fades down."* Both are one state driven from the work. The screen's transitions are done in the display's shader — the picture scales and blooms inside the glass; the glass, the bezel and the console never move. The spotlight is an emissive lift on the console's own material plus a pool of light on the disc, no shadow-casting light, because this has to run on a phone; it rises in a third of a second and decays over several, so a console still glowing faintly says *this one worked recently*. Virgil's slabs stay on: he is always conducting, and the three wake when called, which is how the system works. **Which collapse was built:** the classic one — vertical to a line, the line to a point, an afterglow. If you meant an iris closing inward, say so and it is one function.

The canonical order, written into the spec so the tube is added to the front of it later and nothing is re-authored: light travels the tube → the console's spotlight lifts → its screen powers on → the work runs → the verdict converges back → the screen collapses off → the spotlight decays.

### "Awaiting review" during a build — a truthfulness defect, fixed at the cause

Your words: *"when the fabricator is building, virgil's screen says awaiting review. its actually awaiting on the build."* You were right, and the fix is not the string. Virgil's slabs no longer carry any fixed label: they are driven from the candidate's state in the constitution's own words, and a test holds every word to the vocabulary and every step to the transition table. Two beats have no word in that vocabulary and are recorded rather than invented for: the rest between loops, when no candidate exists, and a hand-off itself, before the agent starts. The audit found two more of the same kind and fixed them: the candidate slab's phase words (`HAND-OFF`, `BUILD`, `VERIFY`…) were invented and are replaced by the states, and its identity-shaped hex string used to change every 0.7 seconds while judgement proceeded, which pictured a candidate changing under review; it is fixed for the candidate now.

What belongs on the record: the honesty band was true — nothing shown was real — and that did not excuse the content being incoherent. An illustrative demonstration must still depict a sequence that could happen, or the band guards against one falsehood ("this is real") while permitting another ("this is how it works"). That is the ground the Interface Keeper role exists to cover, and you caught it before any reviewer did.
