## What changed, and why

### The consoles behind Virgil's, symmetrical

Your words: *"each console should be behind the main virgil console, one directly behinmd it and the other to the left and right, so its symmetrical."* Virgil's console stays where it was. The Prover's stands directly behind it, square to you; the Fabricator's to the left and the Keeper's to the right, mirrored exactly and turned a little in. Which role took which slot you did not say; the Fabricator, the Prover and the Keeper are left, centre and right in the order of the three hops, and that is this session's choice. **A consequence you should see rather than be told:** with the camera almost level, whoever stands directly behind Virgil is partly behind him from the opening view — you see the Prover's head and shoulders and his console's screen above Virgil's head, and all of him when you drag round. And a symmetrical set is wide where a phone is tall, so on a phone held upright the cast is a band across the middle of a tall frame; all four are in it.

### The consoles' own screens carry the information; the floating panels are gone

Your words: *"each screen of the actual consoles (created by meshy) should have the information on it"*, and, of the treatment, *"make them compleetyley black, reflective, and text sitting slightly under it."* It is the visor technique on a console. A pipeline script finds the screen on each console's own mesh and records which of its triangles it is; at runtime those triangles are copied out and the live screen is drawn on them through the same face and glass that make the visors — one implementation, two hosts. The glass stands twelve millimetres off the screen so the text sits under it and a highlight travels across it.

**What the consoles turned out to carry, measured:** each has exactly one usable screen — a recessed navy panel in a cream bezel at the top, about 0.9 by 0.55 metres, tilted back thirteen degrees, facing you — with pale writing baked into it. The screens are **navy, not black**, the same navy as the trim, so the visor's paint rule could not find them from the texture; they are selected by geometry (a measured region, the facing, the darkness of most of the triangle). And the baked writing means a per-pixel mask would have punched holes through the content, so the whole selected triangle is the screen, which paints over the writing. Your idea worked; the models just did not honour the "pure black" you asked Meshy for, and it did not matter.

The three floating panels are retired. That, and the agents' screens being dark when idle, is what answers *"too many screens"*.

### No window

Your words, twice: *"remove the circular window. I asked for that."* V7 kept the arch as a skyline element and read your first instruction as the window in the wall; that was wrong, and it is gone. The model stays in `assets/models/candidates/` as evidence, and its payload stays in the file because the retired room's window frame still uses it — dropping it would save about 0.9 MB and is one decision away.

### The camera almost level; the planet and the station moved up

Your words: *"The camera starts too high up so everything looks squished. It should start almost levelm with the characters, maybe a tiny bit higher."* Eleven degrees above chest height, not thirty. The orbit stays on a wide arc, and you can rise to look over the set but not sink under the disc. *"the planet and space station in the background is covered by the floating tabletop, so they need to be moved up"* — both are raised so they clear the disc's far edge from the level camera, and they swapped sides so the planet is not behind the controls.

### The screen animations, rebuilt for craft

Your words, three times over: *"they look a little basic … not something that is basic clip art that is animated"*; *"make the animations more... futeristic. put work into those animations, make it look detailed. And don't make the 'recieving' screen so quick"*; *"the information should come back onto the screen and almost converge, or like millions of pieces of data coming to gether."* No words were added. What changed is the motion: every easing has weight, things arrive in sequence, secondary motion trails primary by a beat, and nothing steps or snaps — V6's rule that motion should be "stepped and snappy" is reversed by your direction. The receiving beat is the centrepiece, six seconds in four stages; the return is its inverse, built as a pair so the two read as a cycle. A verdict is an event now — the ring seals, pulses once, the mark draws itself — not a colour change.

**The seam for your tube idea.** *"Maybe later we can have tubes connecting all of the consoles and … light travels across the tube to the correct console, and back again."* Not built, and worth building: it would make a hand-off one continuous event and give the receiving beat its duration honestly. What is built toward it: every receiving and returning animation starts from a **defined arrival point** — the screen edge nearest Virgil, at a recorded moment — kept in one file (`screens/arrival.ts`), which also gives the point in the room where a tube would terminate on each console. A tube can feed into that without the animations being rebuilt. The symmetrical layout is what makes three tubes from one console natural.

### Counters, real vocabulary, and four verdicts

### The evidence beneath a verdict, and the owner gate

Of the four things offered when you asked what else could show what is actually happening, you chose the first and the third. **The evidence** is on Virgil's centre slab under every verdict: a small number of counts whose kinds are the ones this repository really produces — its tests, its typecheck tasks, the Owner Build's requests and off-document requests, the knowledge graph's tethers — because a verdict alone is a claim and the checks that produced it are the proof. **The owner gate** is a new beat: after the Keeper's verdict the candidate is `SAFE_TO_MERGE` — "every merge gate passes. ### The screens dark when idle, on like a television, off like an old one; the consoles spotlit

Your words: *"we should avoid having every screen on if its not in use … when it turns on, make it turn on like a tv, and whene it turns off, an exadurated old tv turning off type animation"*, and *"when they are doing work, they are lit up lighter, almnost as if a spotlight is on them, and when they are done, the spotlitght slowly fades down."* Both are one state driven from the work. What belongs on the record: the honesty band was true — nothing shown was real — and that did not excuse the content being incoherent. An illustrative demonstration must still depict a sequence that could happen, or the band guards against one falsehood ("this is real") while permitting another ("this is how it works").
